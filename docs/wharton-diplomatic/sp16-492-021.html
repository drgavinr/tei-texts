[TNA, SP 16/492/21]

[f. 68r]

Noble Sr

ſeptember the 13th was my last vnto you this euen wee had tidings that [? Killingworth ?]
Castle in warwick ſhire 6 miles from Couentry was taken with store of ammunition and [? money ?]
and ſome priſoners ther number vncertaine the rest fled and the Cuntrey psued them
and wanted but the aſſistance of Couentry to destoy them all wenſday ſep the 14th our
forces [? bothe ?] foote and horſe marched into the feild and the Lord generall veiwed vs both
both front reare and fflancks where the Drumms beatinge and the Trumpets ſounding
made a harmony delectable to our frends but terrible to our enemies this euen
contrary to expectation our regiment marched 5 [...] miles north east vnto
Spratton where wee and as many as could bilited in the towne the rest quartered
the cuntrey Thurſday our regiment met againe where thoſe famous lawes
for our army were read and expounded this day wee received and accepted
ſargeant maior Neale ffryday our regiment were commaunded to meete
here againe to be mustered [...]where we exerciſed in the feild the whole
day and the muster master came not wherat we were all much diſpleaſed
this night our company by lot watched the towne this e ſaturday our
regiment met againe and weere mustered. this euen Captaine ffrancis
returninge from London informed mee of the corragiouſneſſe and constancy
of the City of london and alſo of ther constant ſupplyes of money and
plate and alſo told me that the whole City were now either reall or
constrained roundheads Sabbath day wee peacably inioyed with Mr Obbediah
Sedgwick who gaue us two heavenly ſermons Munday morninge our
regiment began to march towards warwick Shire and paſſed through
west haddon Creeke and hill Morton where wee had a ſuply of drinke
which upon a march is very rare and extreordinary welcome and at the
end of ten miles wee came to Rugby in warwick ſhire where we had
good quarter at this towne Mr Norton formerly preached this towne
alſo was lately diſarmed by the Cauallers on the ſabbath day the
inhabitants beinge at Church Tueſday morninge our Regiment
marched 2 miles vnto Dunſmore heath where the Lord Generall
and his Regiment met vs as alſo the Lord of Stanford Colonell
Cholmley and Colonell Hampden with many troopes of horſe and
18 feild peeces where we kept our randevoes vntill euen where wee
had tidings that all the malignants in worſeſter Shire were with the
Caualleers were got into worſester and fortified themſelues whervpon
wee marched 6 miles vnto baggington within two miles of Couentry this night
the rest of our regiments [? quartered ?] about the cuntrey Thursday Wenſday morninge
wee marched towards warwick leauinge Killingworth Castle vpon the
[? right ?] and after wee had marched 6 miles our forces met againe and [? quarted ?]
before warwick vntill 40 peeces of Ordinance with other carriadges paſſed
by in which time I veiwed the antiquities about one mile on this [? side ?] warwick
as Sr Guyes caue his Chappell and his picture in it his stables all hewed out
of the maine Rock as also his garden and 2 ſpringinge wels wherat he [? drank ?]
as is reported from hence our regiment marched through warwick in
ſuch hast that I could not veiw the towne but had only a ſight of
the Castle which is very stronge built vpon a mighty rock wherof ther
are store in this Cuntrey this night we marched 2 miles further vnto
Burford where our quarter was as [? constantly ?] ſince his [? exelencyes ?] comminge
[? it ?] is very poore for many of our [? ſoilders can get nether beads bread nor water ?]
[f. 68v]
which makes them grow very stronge for backbiters haue bin ſeene to march vpon
ſome of them ſixe on brest and eight deepe at their open order and I feare I
ſhall be in the ſame condition eare longe for wee can get no carriadge for officers
so that my trunck and all neſſeſſaries therin are left at Couentry and indeede
our regiment is more ſlighted then any other which is cheifly occaſioned as I
conceiue partly by the falſe informations of leiftenant Colonell Briddeman
and our late Sargeant major Generall Ballard prophane wretches but cheifly
for want of our Colonell who ſhould be one of the counſell of Warre at which
counſell we have none to pleade for vs or remoue false aſperſions cast vpon
vs in [? so ?] much that I haue heard heard ſome of our captaines repent their
comminge forth and all for want of a Colonell Thurſday morninge we marched
in the front 4 miles towards worceſter where wee met one ridinge post
from worceter informinge vs that our troopes and the Cauallers were ther in fight
but it was falſe only to hast the captaines from Warwick vpon this report
our whole regiment ran ſhoutinge for 2 miles together and cryinge to worcester
to worcester and deſired to march all night but after we had marched 2 miles
further we were commaunded to stand vntill our forces paſſed by and then
marched 2 miles further vnto Aſſincantlo where wee could get no quarter
neither bread nor drinke by reaſon of the Lord Comptons late beinge there
ffryday we marched 4 miles vnto Oster where our forces met and from thence
8 miles and incamped 4 miles on this ſide worſeſter but our ſoildiers cryed
out for one hower together to goe forward to ſet vpon the enemy but could get
no commiſſion this day we had such fowle wether that before I had marched
one mile I was weet to the ſkinne this day our horſe forces namely Sr william
[? belfore ?] Colonell Sands Colonell vines Colonell Clarke Maior Duglas kept all the paſſages
ouer Seuerne and by that meanes kept in the Caualleres who often aſſayed to fly
but were prevented repelled theſe Commaunders ſent to his Exelency for 3 feild peeces
and offered with them to keepe them in on that ſide vntill wee had ſurrounded
them but they were denied this day towards euen prince Robert entered the
City at a bye paſſage with eighteene troopes of horſe most of the City cryinge
welcome welcome but principaley the Maior who deſired to entertaine him but hee
anſwered god damne him he would not stay but would goe waſh his hands in the
blod of the roundheads and immediately ſet ſome to lye in ambuſh and with
the rest ſallyed out vpon the our forces and immediatly Colonell Fines Sands came
on brauely [? euene vnto ?] the brest of their cheife commaunder and diſcharged
the rest vndauntedly followed but ther forces immediatly fled and ours followed
them and by the ambuſhment were beſet before and behind ſo that the batt[...]
was very hot and many fell on both ſides ſome of our cheife commaunders
as Colonell [...] Sands and Duglas was wounded but none ſlaine the and are ſince both dead the cheife amongst
the Caualleeres were Prince Robert who I heare was wounded the Lord
Crauen and the Lord of Northhampton our wounded men they brought into the
City and stripped stabbed and ſlaſſed ther dead bodies in a most barbarous manner
and imbrued their hands in their blode they also at ther returne met a yonge
gentlemen a parlaiment man as I am informed his name I cannot learne and
stabbed him on horſe back with many wounds and trampled vpon him and alſo most
maliciouſly ſhot his horſe this euen our Generalls trope of gentlemen goinge to
quarter themſelues about the cuntrey were betrayed and beſet by the enemy and
ouermuch timerous immediately fled so confuſedly that some brake their horſes [? necks ?]
others their owne ſome were taken others ſlaine and ſcarce halfe of them [? eſſaped ?]
[f. 69r]
which is ſuch a blot vpon them as nothinge but ſome deſperate exploit will wipe of
hearinge this newes wee immediately cryed out to march vnto them and forwith drew
out a forlorne hope ſome out of euery company and ſent them before intendinge to
march after them but about eleuen of the clock the enemies fled and our hope
returned here we aboade all night where we had ſmall comfort for the it rained
hard our foode was fruite for thoſe that could get it our drinke water and beds the
earth our canopy the clouds but we pulled vp the hedges pales and gates and made
good fiers his exelency promiſinge vs that if the cuntrey releiued vs not the day
followinge he would fier ther townes thus we continued ſinginge of pſalmes vntill
the morninge Saturday morninge we marched vnto worſeſter our regiment in
the reare of the waggons the raine continuinge the whole day and the way
so baſe that we went vp to the ancles in thick clay and about 4 of the clock
after noone entred the city where we found where we found 28 dead men
which we buried ſome of them Cauallers and theſe were all that we can find
ſlaine on our ſide this euen by lot our company watched one of the gates and
alſo the day folloinge vntill euen this euen his exelencyes guard entred the
maiors houſe and toke him priſoner who is now more guarded then regarded
Sabbath day morninge our ſoildiers entered a vault of the collidge where his
exelency was to heare a ſermon and found eleuen barrels of gunpowder and a
pot of bullets this day Mr marſhall Sedgwich &c preached about the City but
I beinge vpon the court of guard could not heare them this euen his exelency
proclaimed that no ſoildier ſhuld plunder either church or priuate howſe vpon
paine of death wee ſhortly expect a pitcht battell which if the Cauallers
will but stand will be very hot for we are all much inraged against
them for their babariſmes and ſhall ſhew them little mercy but I want time
to in large my ſelfe to conclude I humbly intreate you to preſent my humble
ſeuice to my Mrs as aſſo Mr Edgertons our quartermaster and mine to Mr Molloyne
and his wife my ſeruice alſo to Mry Hunt Mr Priaux Mr Simpſon and his
wife Mr Hawkins Mr [...] Starkey & I humbly in treate let me heare
from you of your welfare My Mistris and your family Sr it is extreame
hast that makes me compoſe so confuſedly and therfore with my ſeruce
To Mr Elizabeth Anna John and little Sammuell and my loue to all my
fellow ſeruants I rest yours in all good ſeruices vntill death

Nehemiah Wharton

My Captaine preſents you with
his best reſpects and drinkes
to you euery day we ſit at table
together I haue receiued your
feather for which I giue you
humble thankes

Worcester Octob the 2d Sept the 26 1642

[f. 69v]

To the worthy and his much
Honored ffrend Mr George
Willingham [? Marchant ?]
at the golden Anchor
in St Swethins Lane
These preſent
