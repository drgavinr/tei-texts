[TNA, SP 16/492/28]

[f. 80r]

My much honored Mr I humbly greete you

my last vnto you was Sept the 26th in which
there were ſome errors occaſioned partly by my ouerhastineſſe in wrightinge for the bearer
promiſed mee to deliuer it on wenſday sept the 28th partly by the various relations
diſperſed but cheifly by reaſon I was vpon the guard and vpon paine of death durst
not stir from it to ſe or heare any occurrences but I had as good intelligence as the
Citty then afforded for the trewth of thinges was not then knowne the errors are theſe
ffirst I wrote that the Earle of North hampton and the Lord Crauen [...] were with the
Prince but they were not but vpon ſuffitient information they were these Prince Robert Duke
Mawrice the Lord Digby Commiſſary Wilmot Sr Lewes Diues Sr William Ruſſell
and Eſquire Hastings a ſecond was that most of the Curoſeers his Exelencies
trope were cut of but they are ſince returned and but few lost but doe still beare
The aſperſion of cowards the third was that Colonell ſands was dead but both he
and captaine Sands though both wounded yet are still liuinge but that I may not trouble
you with common relations which commonly are fictions I haue conferred with commaunders
the best intelligencers and haue aſſo veiwed the place where the battaile was fought but
breifly Worceſterſhire is a pleaſant fruitfull and rich Cuntrey aboundinge in corne
woods pastures hils and valleyes euery hedge and heigh way beſet with fruite
but eſpecally with peares wherof they make that pleaſant drinke called Perry
which they ſell for a penny a quart though better then euer you tasted at
London Touchinge the City it is more Large then any I haue ſeene ſince I left
London and Like London it abounds in outwarde thinges of all kinds but for want
of the word the people perriſh it is pleaſantly ſeated exceedinge populous and
doubtleſe very rich it is scituate on the east banke of that famous Riuer
Seuerine the wals in the forme of a triangle the gates ſeauen the Bulwarkes
fiue but much decayed no Castle, only a mount of earth in this Citty there is
a very stately Cathedrall called St Maries in which there are many stately monuments
but amongst the rest in the middle of the quire is the monument of Kinge
John all of white marble with his picture theron to the life on the South ſide
Kinge Arthurs Tombe of Jette but no picture theron this Citty hath alſo a
stronge stone Bridge ouer ſeuerne conſiſtinge of ſixe arches with a gate
on the middle of the bridge as stronge as that on London Bridge with a pcullis
fiue miles upon the Left of this is Maluern hills which for height and
length and breadth doe many degres exceede all that euer I ſee
I nominate them becauſe they are famous for on the top of them there is a
very braue chaſe for many miles together [? a ?] large ditch and ſprings
alſo all on the very top betweene theſe hils and the riuer was the late
ſkermish about one mile from the City which was on this manner Our troopes
on that ſide were deuided to keepe the ſeuerall paſſages and Colonell
Sands Colonell vines and sargeant Maior Duglas with their Troopes were
set to keepe this paſſage which was at the end of a narrow lane
which the treacherous inhabitants neare them diſcouered to the Prince
and he forwith ſet ſome troopes to lye in ambuſh who brake [? downe ?] a
hedge and lay in a feild on the right of them the treacherous citizens attendinge
them in multituds with muskets who lay on each ſide the hedge this done
the prince with other forces went to meete them but first ſent a false alaram
[f. 80v]
informinge them that the Caualeers weere all fled and had left the City and alſo
that his exelency was come euen to the gates and immediately theſe 3 troopes
poasted away to meete his Exelency for Sr william Belfore Colonell Clarke and
Our other commaunders heard not of it But hastinge downe A narrow lane
the prince met them Colonell Sands beinge in the front the Prince aſked him
whom he was for, he anſwared for Kinge and Parlaiment he replyed not for
the kinge alone he anſwared no then ſaid the Prince for the Kinge haue
at you the Colonell anſwared for the Parlaiment haue at you ad ſo
they diſcarged each at other the rest followed them and preſently thoſe
in ambuſh fired vpon them as alſo the muſqetteers behind the hedges the
horſemen charged not the ſecond time but immediatly fell to their ſwords
ſo that for the ſpace of almost an hower the ſkermiſh was excedinge hot
this relation I had from an gentleman that was in the front of the battell
and was wounded in with the ſword and bullets in ſeuen ſeuerall places was
stripped naked and yet liueth he affirmeth that though there were
ten for one yet there were more ſlaine and wounded of them then
of us and I ſpake with one that heard the Prince at his returne
ſay that our men fought more like Lions then men our wounded
commaunders he brought into the City and gaue commaund that
that they ſhould be carefully looked vnto and with all ſpeed gathered
his forces together and fled leauinge all their reconings to pay in ſome
places 20 pounds in others ten in ſome more ſome leſſe pretendinge and
alſo promiſinge to returne ſince their departure we heare that the
Prince is wounded but it is certaine Duke Mawrice is mortally wounded
they report vnto the Kinge that they have ſlaine eight hundred of
our men when there were but 28 ſlaine in all and ſome of them were
Caualleeres of this I am certaine for I told all their graues and
ſearched the register of St Jones parrish where they were all buried
they boast wonderfully and ſweare most helliſhly that the next time
they meete vs they will make but a mouthfull of vs but I am
pſwaded the Lord hath giuen them this ſmall victory that they
may in the day of battell come on more preſumptuouſly to ther owne
destruction in which battell though I and many thouſands more may bee
cut of yet I am confident the Lord of hoasts will in the end
triumph gloriouſly ouer theſe horſes and all their Curſed riders
They left at their flight in the City some horſes Truncks and other
thinges all which his exelency hath ſeazed on Colonell Eſſex is
made gouernor of this City and his regiment ſhall be gariſons here
Sr Our army did little thinke euer to haue seene Worcester but the
prouidence of god hath brought vs heither and had it not the City
is ſo vile and the cuntrey ſo baſe ſo papisticall and Atheisticall
and Abominable that it reſembles ſodom and is the very Embleme of
Gomorah and doubtleſſe it would haue bin worſe then either Algiers
or Malta a very den of theeues and a receptacle and refuge for all
[f. 81r]
the helhounds in the Cuntrey I ſhould haue ſaid in the land but wee haue
handſomley handled ſome of them and doe cull out the rest as fast as wee
can who verbally cry pecaui but cordeally itterum faciam and indeed
they doe as they are taught by Dr Prediux late madde Byshop and
other popiſh preists who are all run away. to conclude Munday Sept 26th
one of Prince Roberts Trumpeters cames to our gates and ſounded a
point of warre who was preſently taken and led blind fold through the
City vnto his Exelency This night Sargeant Maior Duglas was nobly
buried Tueſday our ſoildiers by commiſſion from his exelency [? marched 7 ?]
miles vnto Sr William Ruſſels houſe and pillaged it vnto the [? bare ?]
walls Wenſday wee fasted and Mr Obbediah Sedgwich preached vnto vs
whom the lord extreordineryly aſſisted ſo that his doctrine wrought
wonderfully vpon many of vs and doubtleſſe hath fitted many of vs for
death which wee all ſhortly expect Thurſday his Exelency proclaimed
that whoſoeuer had any goods of the [? Caualleres ?] in custody ſhould
forthwith ſurrender them this day I mett with your ſeuant Dauy who
is in good health with his horſe he was on the west of Seuerne neare
the battell but vntill it was ended heard not of it wee ioyntly preſent
you and my Mrs with our humble ſeruice as alſo Mr Willingham your
Brother with his family Mr Chappels man and I doe preſent our ſeruice [? to ?]
Mr Chappell and his wife Mr ffelton and his wife and deſire [? to heare ?]
of their welfare ffryday morninge our drumms beate for our
Regiment to march away but wheither I know not wee ſhould bee
very glad to ſee our Colonell Sr I humbly intreate to excuſe my
late errors and to accept theſe my pore indeauors beinge the last
I ſuppoſe that euer I ſhall preſent vnto you thus with my
dearest loue to all your children and my fellow ſeruants wheither
with you and or from you beinge to march away in hast I must conclude

your everlouinge humble thankfull and auntient ſeruant

Nehemiah Wharton

Worcester Sept
the 30th 1642

[f. 81v]

To the worthy and his much
Honored ffrend Mr George
Willingham at the
Golden Anchor in
Sweethins Lane
London
