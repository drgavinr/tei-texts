Couentry northamton September the 7th 1642

Worthy Sr

I preſent you and my Mrs with my humble ſeuice
wiſhinge you and all yours the constant fruition of all hapineſſe
this is my fifth letter vnto you but I question wheither
euer you receiued one of them becauſe you neiuer yet honored
mee with a peece of paper I have therfore once more ſent vnto
you by this bearer Leiftenant Wade who hath promiſed to
deliuer theſe with his owne hands our place of randeuoues
is still at North hampton which for Cituation circuit
and statelyneſe of buildinge exceeds couentry but the walls
are miſerably ruined though the cuntrey abounds in mindes
of stone from hence I haue nothinge remarkeable to preſent
you with neither will time permit me to acquaint you with
circumstances this morninge our regiments beinge drawne
unto the feild to exerciſe many of them diſcouered their
baſe ends in vndertakinge [? this ?] deſeigne and demaunded fiue
ſhillinge a man which they ſay was promiſed them monthly
by the comitte or they would ſurrender their armes
whervpon Colonell hamden and other commaunders labored
to appeaſe them but could not ſo that if they haue not
the forenamed ſupply we feare a very greate fraction
amongst vs there is alſo greate deſention betweene our Troopers
and foote companies for the foote men are much abuſed and [? ſouldiers ?]
pillaged and wounded I my ſelfe haue lately experimentally [? found ?]
it for they toke from mee aboue the worth of three pounds
but I am not diſcorged by any of theſe but by gods aſſiſſtance
will vndauntedly proceede for god is able to reconcile all our
diferences Sr I humbly intreate you to ſend mee a ſmall feather
white and one tip of black by one Gregory Kent knowne
vnto mr Molloyne and [? beinge ?] neare him he will be with mee
by Saturday at night I ſhall be very ioyfull at the last
to heare of the welfare of you all thus co[...]inge you
into the custody of the most heigh for [...] present I
rest

Your humble ſeruant Till Death

Nehemiah Wharton

The bearer stayed the writinge therof which is the cause
my rustick ſcriblinge and inditinge

[f. 9v]

To his much Honored ffrend
Mr George Willingham
Marchant at the
Golden Anchor
Swethins Lane
