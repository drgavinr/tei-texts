[TNA, SP 16/491/138]

[f. 345r]

Couentry August the 30th 1642

Noble Sr

as time pmits I deſire to acquaint you with the paſſages
of my pilgramage my last vnto you was from Couentry August the 26th
which place is still our quarter a City inuironed with a wall coequall if not
excedinge that of London for breadth and height the compaſe of it
is neare 3 miles all of free stone it hath 4 stronge gates stronge
battlements stored with towers bulwarkes courts of guard and other
neſſeſſaries this City hath magnificent churches and stately streetes
with in it ther are also ſeuerall ſweete and plaſant ſpringes of water
built of freestone very large ſuffitient to supply many thousand men
this City gates are guarded day and night with 400 armed men
and no man entreth in or out but opon examination Thursday
it is also very ſweetly Cituate Thurſday august the 26th our
ſoildiers pillaged a malignant fellowes houſe in this City
and the Lord brooke immediately proclaimed that whoſoeuer
ſhould for the future offend in that kind ſhould haue Martiall
law this day commaund that was giuen that all ſoildiers
ſhould attend their colors every morne by ſixe of the clock
to march into the feild to practiſe which is done accordingly
ffryday ſeuerall of our ſoildiers both horſe and foote ſallyed
out of the City vnt[? o ?] the Lord Dunſmores parke and brought
from thence greate store of Veniſon which is as good
as euer I tasted and I euer ſince they make it their
dayly practiſe ſo that veniſon is almost as common
with vs as beefe with you this day our horſemen ſallyed
out as their dayly custome is and brought in with them two
Caualeeres and with them an old baſe preist the parſon
of So[? we ?] neare vs and led him rediculouſly about the
City vnto the cheife Commaunders this day I againe meet
with Dauy your horſeman and both horse and man are in good caſe
Saturday I met with your auntient maide ſeruant
Lydea with her father and her brethren who all wept
for ioy when they ſaw mee an officer in this Deſeigne
for the day wee marched into Couentry the Rebels had
command to pillage Anstey the habitation of her
father and Bromagam the place of her and her
husbands dwellinge both which are but 3 miles
from Couentry ſhe hath bin maried 3 yeares
[f. 345v]
and preſents her ſeruice vnto you and my mistris and your children
her father inuited mee and 20 of the cheife of my company
to dinner at Anstey but time would not ſuffer vs to
accept it This day a whore which had followed our
Campe from London was taken by the ſoildiers and
first led about the City then ſet in the pillory after
in the cage then duckt in a riuer and at the last
baniſht the City Sunday morne the Lord of Eſſey
his Chaplaine Mr Kemme the Coopers ſonne preched
vnto vs and this was the first sermon we heard
ſince wee came from Alisbury but before he had ended
his first prayer newes was brought in to the Church
unto our Commaunders that Noneaton ſome ſixe miles
from vs was fired by the enemy and forthwith our
Generall and ſeuerall Captaine iſſued forth but I and
many others stayed vntill ſermon was ended after
which wee were commaunded to march forth with
all ſpeede namely my Captaine with Captaine Beacon
and Captaine ffrancis of our regiment and of other
regiments in all to the number of 1000 foote and one troope
of horſe but before wee came at them they ran all
away not hauinge done much harme whervpon wee
returned to Couentry againe with a commaund to
be ready in armes by fiue in the morninge next
vpon paine of death our men are very corragious
and that they may ſo continue wee deſire accordinge
to promiſe a ſupply of faithfull able miniſters which
wee excedingly want this morninge wee are
marchinge forth to pſue the rebels and that we may
obtaine victory we againe deſire your earnest and
constant prayers Thus with my humble ſeruice to
your ſelfe and my Mistris and alſo vnto all
[f. 346r]
Your Children and alſo my loue to all my fellow
ſeruants whether with you or from you for the preſent
in extreame hast I rest but not to remaine
till death

Your humble Louinge and
thanckfull ſeruant

Nehemiah Wharton

I pray preſent my most humble ſeruice
vnto Mr Molloyne & Mr William Willingham &
my Aunt Mr Hawkins Mr Rudduck
Mr Kinſman Mr Watſon and the rest
of my louinge frends and acquaintance

I humby intreate you fauorably to accept theſe rude
lines for I want time either to write or compoſe
them in a more comely forme I earneſtly
deſire to heare of the occurrences of London

[f. 346v]

To his much Honored
ffrend Mr George
Willingham at the
Golden Anchor in
St Swethins
Lane Marchant
