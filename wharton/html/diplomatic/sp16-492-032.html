[TNA, SP 16/492/32]

[f. 87r]

most worthy Sr

ffryday Sept the 30th was my last vnto you. this day a company of knights [? gentle
men ?] and yeomen, of the County of Hereford, came to his Exelency, petioners for strength
to be ſent ſpedily to Hereford. and forthwith wee were commaunded to draw out 15 men
out of euery company in our Regiments, in all about 900, with 3 troopes of horſe and
2 peeces of Ordinance. with which we marched (a forlorne hope) towards Hereford.
our leaders were The Earle of [? Stanfords ?] Leiftenant Colonell, Sargeant maior Barrif, and Captain
Ingliſby, of Colonell Hampdens Re, Captaine Iones of Cholmleys Re, Captaine Ward of Stanfords,
Captaine Pouy of Hollis his regiment. beſides Leiftenants and Sargeants. Our ministers
Mr John sedgwick, and Mr Kemme. after wee had marched 10 miles wee came to Bromyard,
the wether wet, and they way very fowle, here we got a little refreſhment and from hence marched
10 miles further to Hereford. but very late before wee got thither. and by reaſon of the raine and
ſnow and extreamity of could one of our ſoildiers died by the way. and it is wonderfull wee did
not all periſh, for the cowardly Caualleers were within [? four ?] miles of vs. in this poore condition
comminge to Hereford the gates were ſhut against vs, and for two howers we stood in dirt
and water, up to the midde legges for the City [? were ?] all malignants, ſaue three which were
roundheads. and the marqueſſe of Harford had ſent them word the day before, that they ſhould
in no wiſe let vs in. or if they did we would plunder their houſes, murder ther children,
burne their Bibles, and vtterly ruinate all. and promiſed he would releive them himſelfe,
with all ſpeede for which cause the Citizens were reſolued to oppoſe vs vnto the death, and
hauinge in the City three peeces of ordinance, charged them with nayles stones &c , and placed
them against vs, and wee against them, reſoluinge either to enter the City, or dye before it.
but the roundheads in the City, one of them an Alderman, Surnamed Lane: pſwaded the
ſilly maior, (for ſo hee is indeede) that his Exelency and all his forces weere at hand. whervpon
he opened vnto vs, and we entred the City at Bysters gate but. found the Dores ſhut, many
of the people with their children fled, and had enoffe to doe to get a little quarter but the
poore maior (ſeinge he was so handſomly Cozoned) was not a little angry. for Harford with his
forces, which ffled from Sherbon, promiſed to viſit them the day followinge. this night
though weet and weary, wee were faine to guard the City. this day our generall proclaimed,
that all delinquents that in ten daies would returne, and ioyne with the parlaiment, ſhould
fauorably, but conditionally, be excepted. exceptinge ſuch as are members of the houſe,
which must ſubmit to the ſenſure therof. But theſe by name are pticularly excepted
The Earle of Bristo, the Earle of Cumberland, the earle of Newcastle, the Earle Riuers,
secretary Nicolas, Mr Endymeon Porter, Mr Edward hide, The Duke of Richmond, the
Earle of Carnaruan, the Lord viſcount Newwarke, The Lord viſcount ffauckland,
his maiesties secretary in ſpetiall. This night thought weet and weary we were faine to guard
the City Saturday our Squadron watced at St Owens gate, which day I tooke an
opportunity to veiw the City, which is well ſcituate, and ſeated upon the riuer [...] Y.
inuironed with a stronge wall, better then any I haue ſeene before, with 5 gates and
a stronge stone bridge of ſixe arches, ouer the riuer, ſurpaſſinge worſeſter. in this
City there is the statlyest market place in the Kingdome. built with Cullumnes, after
the maner of the Exchange. the Minster euery way exceedinge that at worcester.
but the City in Circuit not ſo large. the inhabitants are totally ignorant in the waies
of god and much addicted to drunkenneſſe, and other vices, but principally vnto
ſwearinge ſo that the children that haue ſcarce learned to ſpeake, doe [? vniverſally ?]
ſweare stoutly. many here ſpeake welsh. this day our companies exerciſinge in the
feilds at worſeſſter, one of The Lord Generals soildiers ſhot at randum, and with
a brace of bullets, ſhot one of his fellow ſoildiers through the head, who
immediatly dyed,
[f. 87v]
Sabbath day, about the time of morninge prayer, we went to the Minster, where
the pipes played, and the puppets ſange ſo ſweetely, that ſome of our ſoildiers
could not forbeare dauncinge, in the holy quire: wherat the Baallists were ſore diſpl[? esd ?]
the Antheam ended they fell to prayer, and prayed deuoutly, for the Kinge, the Biſhops &c
and one of our ſoildiers with a loud voice ſaide, what neiuer a bit for the Parlaimen
which offended them much more. not ſatiſfied with this humane ſeruice, we went to
deuine, and paſſinge by found ſhops open, and men at worke. to whom wee gaue ſome
plaine dehortations, and went to heare Mr Sedgwick, who gaue us two famous
ſermons: which much affected the poore inhabitants, who wonderinge ſaid, they neiuer
heard the like before. and I beleeue, them, the Lord mooue your harts to commiſerate
[? ther ?] diſtreſſes, and to ſend them ſome faithfull and painfull ministers, for the reuennews
of the Collidge, will maintaine many of them. This euen the Earle of Stanford
(who is made gouernor of the City Hereford) entred the City, with a Regiment of
foote, and ſome troopes of horſe: and tooke vp the Biſhops Pallace, for his quarter. and is
resolued, there to abide whervpon On Munday morninge, we marched towards
Worcester, and at the end of ten miles came to Bromyard, where we quartered all
night, This day his Exelency proclaimed, that all ſoildiers that would ſet to didginge
ſhould haue twelue pence the day, and enter into pay preſently. Tueſday wee marched
to worcester, and weere received with much ioy. for the Deſeigne was ſo
deſperate, that our Iuditious frends, neiuer looked to see vs againe. I am in
good health, and by the goodneſſe of god ſupplyed with strengh, beyond expectation.
Wenſday morninge, I went to veiw the ſoildiers workes, who haue pourtraed out
the ſeuerall formes of their Scaunces, halfe moones, Redouts, &c beginninge at
Seuerne on one ſide, of the City, and goe round the City vnto Seuerne againe.
it will be finished with all conuenient ſpeede. for as wee intrench here ſo
alſo doth Prince Robber, at Bridge norton, twelue miles of. and we heare at
Beaudle alſo, from whence commeth the coales, that ſupply Our City. but we haue
ſent out ſome forces to expell them thence. this day after noone, at the request
of my Captaine and other commaunders: I againe rode with them vnto Maluerne
hils, forementioned, leauinge our horſes in the towne, after much toyle we aſſcended
the hills, which indeede are a very Curious proſpecte for the day beinge cleare,
wee could see neare thirty miles round, all the valley betweene vs and woster,
though fiue miles broad, and many miles longe, (incloſed with hedges) ſemed vnto
vs, but as a garden with a few knots in it, (though indeede they are very large
pastures) at the bottome of theſe hils is Maluerne Church, the statlyest
parriſh Church in England. adorned with varieties of rarities, which I
want time to expreſſe. Thurſday wee exerciſed our ſoildiers in the feilds, this day
The Lord of Coventry came in to vs. and this euen we expected 3 lords more with [? a ?]
Meſſage from his maiesty, ffryday a pare of gallowes were ſet up: in the market place,
for the villan that betrayed our troopes, [? auoide ?] I
haue breifly ſent you euery dayes, paſſage ſince I left London which I hope you
haue receiued. I earnestly deſire to heare of the welfare of you, my Mrs, and
[f. 88r]
Your whole family, by this bearer, Thomas Weeden, who weekely viſits our army, and
is faith full. he lodgeth at the Saracens head, in Carter lane. Sr I greete you and
my Mrs with my most humble ſeruice, as alſo Mr Molloyne and his Wife, vnto whom
I am much ingaged principally for his deuine exortation, to reade thoſe 2 chapters
which I and my company haue often done, they beinge very ptinent to the deſeigne
in hand. I pray excuse my longe ſilence towards him and assure him that I will pticularly preſent
him, with the next remarkeable paſſage. I would haue done it before this time,
but your Athenians catch it from vs, and carry it post vnto you, (though for the most
more then is true) that before we can get conueyance, it is turned into antiquity
thus with my loue and ſeruice to Mrs Elizabeth, Anna, John, and little Sammuell, and my
loue to all my fellow ſeruants, of both ſexes, for the preſent I rest, but not ſeaſe
to remaine vntill death:

Your auntient, humble, and affectionate ſeruant,

Nehemiah Wharton

Worcester
Octob the 7th 1642
