Alisbury August the 16th 1642

Worthy Sr

on Munday August the 8th we marched to Acton but beinge
the 6th company we were belated and many of our ſoildiers were [? constrained ?]
to lodge in beds whoſe feathers were aboue a yard longe Tueſday early
in the morninge ſeuerall of our ſoildiers inhabitinge the out parts
of the towne ſallied out vnto the houſe of one Penrudduck a
papist and beinge baſely affronted by him and his dogge entred his
houſe and pilladged him to the purpoſe this day alſo the ſoildiers
got into the Church defaced the auntient and ſacred glaſed picturs
and Burned the holy railes Wenſday Mr Loue gaue vs a famous
ſermon this day also the ſoildiers brought the holy railes from
Chiſſick and burned them in our towne at chiſſick they also intended
to pillage the Lord of Portlands houſe and alſo Doctor Ducke
but by our Commanders they were preuented This day our genell ſoldiers
generally manifested their diſlike of our Leiftenant Colonell
who is a Godamme blade and doubtleſſe hatch in Hell and wee
all deſire that ether the Parlaiment would depoſe him or God
conuert him or the Divill fetch him a way quick This day
Towards euen our Regiment marched to Vxbridge but I wa[...]
left behind to bringe vp 30 men with ammunition the next mornin[...]
Thurſday I marched towards Vxbridge and at Hillington one mile
from Vxbridge the railes beinge gone wee got the ſurplaſſe to
make vs handcherchers and one of our ſoildiers ware it to vxbridge
this day the Railes of Vxbridge formerly remoued was with the
ſeruice boock burned this euen Mr Hardinge gaue vs a [? werthy ?]
ſermon ffryday I with 3 other commaunders were ſent with
100 Muſqetteres to bringe the ammunition to Amerſam in
Buckinham shire which is the ſweetest Country that euer
I ſaw and as is the cuntrey ſo also is the people but
wantinge roome for the Regiment comminge after vs we were
constrained to march 4 miles further vnto greate Miſſenden
where we had noble entertainment from the whole towne but
espetially from Sr Brian Ireſon and the minister of the towne
Satturday morninge our companies overtoke vs and we marched
together vnto Alſberry and after we had marched [? a longe mile ?]
[f. 265v]
ffor ſo they are all in this Cuntrey vntill wee came to Wendeuer
where wee [? looted ?] refreſſhed ourſelues burnt the railes and accidentally
one of Captaine ffrancis his men forgettinge he was charged with a
bullet ſhot a maide through the head and ſhe immediately died
from hence wee marched very ſadly 2 miles wher Colonell
Hamden accompanied with many Gentlemen well horſed met vs
and with greate Ioy ſaluted and welcomed vs and conducted vs
vnto Alſbury where we haue a Regiment of foote and seuerall
Troopes of horſe to ioyne with vs in this towne our welcome
is ſuch that wee want nothinge but a good Leiftenant Colonell
Sabath day August the 15 in this towne a pulpit was built in the
market place where wee heard 2 worthy ſermons this eueninge
our vngodley Leiftenant Colonel vpon an vngrounded whimsey commaunded
2 of our Captaines namely Captaine ffrancis and Captaine Beacon
with their companies to march out of the towne but they went
not euery day our ſoildiers by stealth doe visit papists houſes
and constraine from them both meate and money they giue them
whole greate loues and Cheſſes which they triumphantly carry
away vpon the points of their ſwords I humbly intreate you
as you deſire the ſucceſſe of our Iust and honorable cause
that you would indeauor to Roote out our Leiftenant Colonell
for if wee march further vnder his commaund we feare vpon
ſuffitient grounds wee are all but dead men Munday August the 16th
Colonell Hamden marched out of Aliſbury with 400 muſquetters
and about an hundred Horſe towards [? wattleton ?] in Oxfordſhire
where the Commiſſion of aray was this day to be ſetled but they
hearinge of the [? aproch ?] of our troopes fled and our horſe
pſued them ſo cloſe that Sr John Curſam was taken and
the lord of [? Barkes ?] was Constrained to take Aſgate [? houſ ?] ſometime
the Lord [? of ?] Carnarvans where our companies haue at this
preſent beſet him and thus much for the preſent: touchinge
Leiftenant Colonell Biddeman formentioned I once [? more ?] humbly
beseech you and not I alone but many others both commaunders
[? officers ?] and [? common ?] ſoildiers that you would [? indeuor ?] to Rout him
[? There ?] are ſeueral other circumstances which I want time to
expreſſe for the office aſſigned mee is very troublesome and
[f. 266r]
Continually takes vp my whole time and therfore for the
preſent let these ſatiſfie. to conclude I preſent you [...]
and miſtris with my humble ſeruice and doe giue you humble
and harty thankes for all your [? former and ?] late fauors and
doe intreate you to remember my humble ſeruice to Mr Molloyne
and wife my ſeruice also to [? Elizabeth ?] Anna John and ſammuell
which I often thinke vpon. and alſo my loue to all my [...]w
ſeruants In extreame hast I rest

Your poore auntient humble and
affectionate ſeruant to commaund

Nehemiah Wharton

[f. 266v]

To the worthy and his
much Honored ffrend
Mr George Willingham merchant
at the golden
Anchor in St Swethins
Lane
