[f. 309r]

Couentrey August the 26th 1642

Worthy Sr

august the 17 our companies after they had taken ſixe
delinquents and ſent them to London returned to Aliſbury this day
wee receiued [? 6 ?] feild peeces and 2 troopes of horse with other neſſeſſaries for warre wenſday morninge
a warwick ſhire minister which the Calualleres had pillaged to the
ſkin gaue vs a ſermon after noone our regiment marchd in to the
feild and ſkirmished Thurſday morninge another ſermon was
giuen us after noone our [? 4 ?] regiment marched in to the feild but by
reson of foule weather were immediatly diſmiſſed this night our
regiment were commaunded to march the next morninge by 4 of the
clock vnder our leiftenant Colonell but our ſargeants resolued
to ſurrender their halberts and the ſoildiers their armes and not
to march ffryday very early in the morninge our leiftenant
Colonell was Caſhered for which I giue you harty thankes and
ſargeant Maior Quarles imployed in his roome wherat both
commaunders officers and ſoildiers excedingly reioyſed this morninge
wee cherfully marched towards Buckingham in the reare of
Colonell Chomleys [...] regiment by reaſon wherof wee could get
no quarter there but were constrained to quarter our ſelues about
the cuntrey whervpon I and 3 gentlemen of my company
viſited that thrice noble gentleman Sr Richard Ingliſby
where his owne table was our quarter and Sargeant Maior
Barrif and his ſonne captaine Ingliſby and ſeuerall other noble
gentlemen were our comrades ſaturday early in the morninge
I departed hence and gathered a compleate file of my owne men
about the cuntrey and marched to Sr Alexander Dentons [? his ?]
parke who is a malignant fellow and killed a fat buck fastened
his head vpon my Halbert and commanded 2 of my pickes
to bringe the body after mee to Buckingham with a guard
of muſqetteeres comminge theither with part of it I feasted
my Captaine captaine Parcker Captaine Beacon and Colonell
Hamdens ſonne and with the rest ſeuerall Leiftenants
Enſeignes and ſargeants and had much thankes for my pains this
day our ſargeant Maior Generall came vnto vs and declared the
commaund giuen him ouer our Regiments Sunday morninge wee
marched from Buckingham in to northampton ſhire a longe and
tedious iorney wantinge both bread and water and about 10 at
night came [? vnto ?] Byfeild in diſpight of our enemies at which
[f. 309v]
Towne we could get no quarter neither meate drinke nor
lodginge and had we not bin ſuplyed with 10 cart loade of
prouiſion and beare from Banbury [...] many of vs had periſhed
this night our company was commaunded to guard the towne all
night which after a longe and tedious march vnto mee was
very greuious Muday morninge wee marched in to
Warwick ſhire with about 3000 foote and 400 horſe
vntill we came to Southum in the way we tooke 2 Calualler
Spies this is a very malignant towne both miniſter ad people
we pillaged the minister and tooke from him a drum and ſeueral
armes this night our ſoildiers wearied out quartered themſelues
about the towne for foode and lodginge but before we could
eate or drinke an alarum cryed arme arme for the enemy
is comminge and in halfe an hour all our ſoildiers though diſperſd were [? compleate ?]
in armes ready to encounter the enemy cryinge out for a diſh of
calualleres to ſupper our horſe were quartered about the cuntrey
but the enemy came not whervpon our ſoildiers cryed out to haue a
breakefast of Cauallers. we strongly barecadoed the towne and
at euery paſſage placed our ordinance and watched it all night
our ſoildiers contented to lye vpon hard stones in the morninge
early our enemies conſiſtinge of about eight hundred horſe
and three hundred foote horse with ordinance led by the Earle of
north hampton the lord of Carnaruan and the Lord Compton &
captaine Legge and others intended to ſet vpon vs before wee could
gather our companies together but beinge ready all night early
in the morninge wee went to meete them with a few troopes of
horſe and ſixe feild peeces and beinge on fier to meete be at
them wee marched thorow the corne and got the hill
of them whervpon the played vpon vs with ther ordinances but
they came ſhort our gunner tooke ther owne bullet ſent it to them
againe and killed 2 horſe and 2 men after we gaue them
eight ſhot more whervpon all their foote companies fled
and offered ther armes in the townes adiacent for twelue
pence a peece ther p troopes whelinge about toke vp ther
[f. 310r]
Tooke vp their dead bodies and fled but the horſe they
left behind them ſome of them hauinge ther [? guts ?] beaten
out on both ſides the number of men ſlaine as themſelus report
was fifty beſides horſe. one drummer beinge dead at the
bottom of the hill our Knapſack boyes rifled to the ſhirt
which was very lowzy an other drummer we fownd 2 miles
of with the his armes ſhot of and lay a diinge ſeuer[...] dead
corps wee found in corne feilds and amongst them [? a ?] trumpeter
whose trumpet our horſmen ſounded into Couentry wee toke ſeueral
priſoners and amongst them Captaine Legge and captaine
Clarke from thence we marched valiantly after them toward
Couentry and at dunſmore heath they threatned to
giue vs battaile but we got the hill of them ordered our
men and cryed for a messe of Calualleres to ſupper as wee
had to breake fast but they all fled and we immediately
marched vnto Couentry where the Countrey met vs and
in armes and welcommed vs and gaue vs good quarter
both for horſe and foote in this battell I met with your
horſeman Dauy and he and I preſent you and my Miſtris
with our most humble ſeruice deſiringe you to pray for vs
and doubt not but both of will valiantly fight the Lords
battaile thus with my seruice to Mrs Eelizabeth Anna
John and Samuell and my loue to all my fellow ſeruants
I rest

Yours in all good ſeruices

Nehemiah Wharton

I pray preſent the humble ſeruice of Gregory Kent and my ſelfe vnto Mr
Molloyne with my best reſpects to Mr Henry Hickman and
informe him that I haue receiued his booke and ſhall acknowledge
my ſelfe ingaged for the ſame I would haue written vnto him and
others my kindred and frends but I want time and therfore
must humbly in treate you to informe them therof I earnestly
deſire to heare from you whether you receiued a letter dated August the
17th or [? not ?]

[f. 310v]

To his much honored frend
Mr George Willingham
marchant at the
golden Anchor in
St Swethins Lane
